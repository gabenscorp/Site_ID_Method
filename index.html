Site Identification tool · Scoring Methodology

How a parcel becomes a suitability score

Every candidate site is scored on fourteen criteria, grouped into four domains, and combined into a single composite suitability score from 0 to 100%. This page shows exactly how — from raw measurement to final percentage — so the result can be read, questioned, and trusted.

OVERVIEW

Four steps, raw data to score

The method is a two-level weighted average. Criteria are weighted within their domain; domains are weighted into the composite. Nothing is a black box — every number traces back to a measurement.

STEP 1

Scoring each criterion 1–5

Every criterion is converted from its raw measurement to a 1–5 score against thresholds set for that criterion. The direction depends on the criterion: for access factors closer is better; for constraints like floodplain or slope, less is better.

STEP 2

Grouping criteria into four weighted domains

The 14 criteria belong to four domains. Within each domain, criteria carry their own weights (shown as bars) and combine by a weighted average into a single 1–5 domain score.

STEP 3

Combining domains into the composite

The four domain scores are combined by a second weighted average. These domain weights express the organisation's siting priorities — they are the main lever for what "suitable" means.

Composite=Σ (domain weight × domain score)÷Σ domain weights
A weighted average (divided by the total weight) — so the composite always stays on the same 1–5 scale, whatever the weights.
STEP 4

Rescaling to a 0–100% score

The 1–5 composite is rescaled to a percentage so it reads intuitively. A composite of 1 (worst possible) maps to 0%; a composite of 5 (best possible) maps to 100%.

A CLOSER LOOK

How the Social domain is scored

Social factors are a fully scored domain — weighted into the composite like the others, not a pass/fail gate. Each of its four criteria is scored 1–5, but the direction differs by facility: some are amenities a site should be near, one is best kept at a distance, and one is a simple yes/no.

Closer is better · amenity
Schools & Technical schools

Scored on distance to the nearest facility — proximity is desirable, so a shorter distance scores higher (near → 5, far → 1).

Farther is better · avoid
Hospitals

Scored on distance to the nearest hospital — a buffer is preferred, so a greater distance scores higher (far → 5, near → 1).

Binary · presence
Cemeteries

A simple presence test: a cemetery on the parcel scores 1; no cemetery scores 5. There is no in-between.

Combined
Social domain score

The four criterion scores combine by weighted average into the Social domain's 1–5 score — which is then weighted into the composite alongside the other three domains.
